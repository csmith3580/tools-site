How to Write a Resume That Gets Past ATS Systems

By the Ignitool team July 20, 2026 8 min read

More than 75% of resumes are rejected by automated screening software before a human recruiter ever reads them. If you've been sending applications and hearing nothing back, this is likely why — not your qualifications, but how your resume is formatted and written for the robots that screen it first.

What is ATS and why does it matter?

ATS stands for Applicant Tracking System — software used by over 99% of Fortune 500 companies and most mid-size employers to manage job applications. When you submit a resume online, it almost never goes directly to a human. Instead, it's parsed by an ATS that extracts information, scores the resume against the job requirements, and decides whether to surface it to a recruiter.

Resumes that score below a threshold — usually around 70-80% match — are filtered out automatically. The recruiter never sees them. This happens regardless of how qualified the candidate is, which is why so many strong candidates get no response from online applications.

A resume optimized for ATS isn't a different document than a resume optimized for humans — it's the same document, written with precision. The keywords are there because they're accurate. The formatting is clean because clear formatting helps everyone.

How ATS systems read your resume

ATS software parses your resume by extracting text and organizing it into categories — contact information, work experience, education, skills. It then compares the extracted content against the job description, looking for keyword matches.

The critical point: ATS systems read plain text, not visual design. A beautifully formatted resume with columns, graphics, and creative fonts may be completely unreadable to an ATS — the software extracts garbled text or misses sections entirely.

⚠️ Common ATS killers: tables and columns (text order gets scrambled), text boxes (often invisible to ATS), headers and footers (often ignored), graphics and icons (cannot be read), unusual fonts (may not render), and PDF files from some design tools (images rather than text).

ATS-safe resume formatting rules

  • Use a single-column layout — two-column resumes get parsed in the wrong order by most ATS systems
  • Standard section headings — use "Work Experience," "Education," and "Skills" — not creative alternatives like "Where I've Made an Impact"
  • Standard fonts only — Arial, Calibri, Georgia, Times New Roman, or Garamond at 10-12pt body text
  • Save as .docx first — Word documents are the most reliably parsed format. Convert to PDF only if the job posting specifically requests it or if you're emailing it directly to a human
  • No tables or text boxes — use standard paragraph text and bullet points only
  • No headers or footers — put your contact information in the main body of the document
  • Standard bullet points — use simple dots (•) or hyphens (-), not decorative symbols

How to optimize your resume for keywords

ATS keyword matching is straightforward: the system looks for words in your resume that appear in the job description. The more matches, the higher your score. Here's how to optimize without keyword stuffing:

Step 1 — Analyze the job description

Read the job posting carefully and identify the keywords that appear most frequently or are emphasized most strongly. Pay particular attention to: required skills and tools, job title and seniority level, industry-specific terminology, and required certifications or qualifications.

Step 2 — Mirror the language exactly

If the job says "project management" — use "project management," not "managing projects." If it says "Google Analytics" — use "Google Analytics," not "web analytics tools." ATS systems match exact phrases, not semantic equivalents.

Step 3 — Include a skills section

A dedicated skills section near the top of your resume gives ATS software a concentrated keyword hit. List tools, technologies, methodologies, and competencies that are directly relevant to the role. Keep it honest — only list skills you can actually demonstrate.

Writing resume bullets that pass ATS and impress humans

Once your resume passes the ATS filter, a human recruiter spends an average of 7.4 seconds scanning it before deciding to read further. Your bullet points need to work for both audiences simultaneously.

The formula that works for both: [Strong action verb] + [what you did] + [measurable result]

❌ Weak — responsibility-focused, no metrics

• Was responsible for managing the company's social media accounts and creating content for various platforms including Instagram, LinkedIn, and Twitter

✅ Strong — achievement-focused, ATS-friendly keywords, measurable

• Grew social media following from 2,400 to 18,000 across Instagram, LinkedIn, and Twitter in 12 months by implementing a data-driven content strategy that increased engagement rate by 340%

The strong version includes more keywords (social media, Instagram, LinkedIn, Twitter, content strategy, engagement rate) and demonstrates real impact — making it both ATS-friendly and compelling to human readers.

The ATS resume checklist

Before you submit — verify every item
✓Single-column layout with no tables, text boxes, or columns
✓Standard section headings (Work Experience, Education, Skills)
✓Keywords from the job description appear naturally throughout
✓Job title in your experience matches (or closely mirrors) the role you're applying for
✓Every bullet point starts with a strong action verb
✓At least 50% of bullets include a specific number or metric
✓Contact info is in the document body, not in a header/footer
✓Saved as .docx (unless PDF specifically requested)
✓No graphics, icons, photos, or decorative elements
✓Spell-checked and proofread — ATS systems flag spelling errors

One more thing — tailor every application

The single most effective ATS strategy is also the most time-consuming: tailoring your resume for each job application. A generic resume that scores 55% against a job description gets filtered out. The same resume, with keywords adjusted to match the specific posting, might score 85% and land in front of a recruiter.

You don't need to rewrite your entire resume for each application. Focus on: updating your professional summary to reflect the specific role, adjusting your skills section to prioritize the tools mentioned in the posting, and tweaking 2-3 bullet points to use the same language as the job description.
