How to Write a Blog Post Title That Ranks on Google

By the Ignitool team July 18, 2026 7 min read

Your blog post title is the single most important line of copy you'll write for any piece of content. It determines whether Google understands what the article is about, whether searchers click your result over the nine others on the page, and whether social media users share it or scroll past. Getting it right is worth the extra ten minutes it takes.

The two jobs every blog title must do

A blog post title serves two masters simultaneously — and most titles fail at least one of them.

Job 1: Tell Google what the article is about. Your title is the strongest on-page SEO signal for the keyword you want to rank for. Google uses it to categorize your content and decide which search queries it should appear for. If your target keyword isn't in the title, you're making Google's job harder and your ranking chances lower.

Job 2: Convince a human to click. Once your article appears in search results, your title (combined with your meta description) determines your click-through rate. A title that ranks on page one but gets a 0.5% CTR generates less traffic than a title ranking on page two with a 5% CTR.

The goal: a title that includes the target keyword naturally AND creates enough curiosity, urgency, or clear value that a searcher chooses your result over every other option on the page.

The step-by-step process for writing a strong blog title

1
Start with the search intent

Before writing a single word, ask: what is someone actually trying to accomplish when they search this keyword? Someone searching "blog title generator" wants a tool. Someone searching "how to write blog titles" wants a guide. Someone searching "blog title examples" wants inspiration. Your title should match the intent precisely.

2
Include the exact keyword near the start

Put your primary keyword as close to the beginning of the title as possible without making it sound unnatural. Google gives more weight to words that appear earlier in the title. "Blog Post Title Ideas: 10 Formulas That Rank" is stronger than "10 Formulas That Rank: Blog Post Title Ideas."

3
Keep it under 60 characters

Google displays approximately 60 characters in search result titles before truncating with "..." Titles that get cut off lose their persuasive impact. Count your characters and trim ruthlessly — every word should earn its place.

4
Add a power element

Include one element that creates desire, curiosity, or urgency: a specific number, a strong adjective, a result, a time frame, or a counterintuitive angle. This is what differentiates your result from the nine competitors on the same page.

5
Write 5-10 options and choose the strongest

Professional copywriters rarely use their first title. Write multiple variations using different formats — how-to, list, question, guide — then choose the one that best balances keyword inclusion, clarity, and click appeal.

The title formats that rank best

The How-To title

How-to titles match the most common informational search intent and signal immediately that the article provides actionable guidance. They rank well because they're specific about what the reader will learn.

✅ Strong how-to title

"How to Write a Blog Post Title That Ranks on Google (Step-by-Step)"

The numbered list

Numbered titles set clear expectations and consistently outperform non-numbered alternatives in CTR tests. They work because the human brain processes lists as more manageable than open-ended articles — the reader knows exactly what they're getting.

✅ Strong list title

"10 Blog Title Formulas That Drive More Organic Traffic"

The ultimate guide

Guide titles signal comprehensiveness and tend to earn more backlinks because other writers reference them as authoritative sources. They work best for broad, evergreen topics where readers want one definitive resource.

✅ Strong guide title

"The Complete Guide to Writing Blog Titles That Get Clicked"

Power words that increase click-through rate

Certain words reliably increase CTR when used naturally in blog titles. Use them sparingly — one or two per title maximum:

  • Proven, tested, data-driven — signal evidence-based content in a world of opinion
  • Complete, ultimate, definitive — signal comprehensiveness
  • Fast, quick, in X minutes — signal low time investment
  • Free, without, no [barrier] — remove perceived obstacles
  • Exact, specific, step-by-step — signal precision and actionability
  • Actually, really, honestly — signal authenticity
