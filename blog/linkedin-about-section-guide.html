How to Write a LinkedIn About Section That Opens Doors

By the Ignitool team July 12, 2026 7 min read

The LinkedIn About section is the most underused piece of professional real estate on the internet. Most professionals leave it blank, fill it with a list of job responsibilities, or paste in their resume summary. Here's how to write one that makes people want to reach out.

Why your LinkedIn About section matters more than you think

When someone visits your LinkedIn profile — a recruiter, a potential client, a hiring manager — the About section is typically the first thing they read after your headline. It's your chance to make a human impression before they scroll through your work history.

LinkedIn's own data shows that profiles with complete About sections receive 40 times more opportunities than profiles without one. Recruiters who use LinkedIn Recruiter search for candidates by keywords — and the About section is one of the primary fields those keywords are pulled from.

Your LinkedIn About section is indexed by Google. Search your own name — your LinkedIn profile likely appears on the first page. That means your About section is often the first thing anyone reads when they Google you professionally.

The most common About section mistakes

Before we get to what works, let's look at what doesn't:

  • Writing in third person. "John is a results-driven marketing professional..." You're on a social network, talking directly to other people. Write in first person — it sounds human.
  • Starting with "I am a..." The weakest possible opener. Everyone on LinkedIn is something. Tell the reader what you do for them, not what you are.
  • Listing job responsibilities. Your experience section already has that. The About section is for the version of yourself that doesn't fit in a job title.
  • No call to action. End every About section with a clear invitation: connect, reach out, visit your website, or email you directly.
  • Leaving it blank. A blank About section signals that you either don't care about your professional presence or haven't thought about what you offer. Neither is the impression you want.

The structure that works

The most effective LinkedIn About sections follow a consistent five-part structure:

1
The hook (first 2 lines)

Only the first 2-3 lines show before "see more." Make them count. Lead with your most compelling point — a result, a mission, a contrarian statement, or the problem you solve.

2
What you do and who you do it for

One to two sentences. Be specific about your specialty and your target audience. "I help B2B SaaS companies build content strategies that drive pipeline" beats "I'm a content marketer."

3
Evidence — specific achievements

Two to three bullet points with real numbers. Not responsibilities — results. "Grew organic traffic from 12K to 180K monthly visitors in 18 months."

4
Your background and perspective

One paragraph on what makes your approach unique. What experiences, skills, or philosophies inform how you work?

5
Call to action

End with a clear invitation. What do you want the reader to do? Connect, message you, visit your website, see your work? Say it directly.

Before and after — a real example

❌ Before — Generic and passive

Results-driven marketing professional with 8+ years of experience in digital marketing, content strategy, and brand development. Skilled in SEO, social media, email marketing, and data analytics. Passionate about helping brands grow their online presence and achieve their business objectives. Looking for new opportunities to leverage my skills in a dynamic environment.

✅ After — Specific and compelling

I turn content into pipeline.

For the past 8 years I've built content programs for B2B SaaS companies that actually move revenue — not just traffic. My work has:

• Grown organic traffic from 12K to 180K monthly visits at a Series B startup
• Generated $2.3M in influenced pipeline through content in 12 months
• Built an email list from 0 to 45,000 subscribers in 18 months

I believe most B2B content fails because it's written for search engines, not for the humans who make buying decisions. I build the bridge between the two.

Currently open to Head of Content and VP Marketing roles at Series A-C SaaS companies. Let's talk: [email]

The second version is longer but more readable. It's specific, human, and ends with a clear action. The reader knows exactly what this person does, what they've achieved, and how to reach them.

How long should it be?

LinkedIn allows up to 2,600 characters in the About section. The sweet spot for most professionals is 200–500 words — long enough to demonstrate substance, short enough to be read in full. Very senior professionals and thought leaders can go longer, but only if every sentence earns its place.

Whatever length you choose, use white space. Short paragraphs and bullet points make text scannable. A wall of text — even great text — discourages reading.

Keywords matter for LinkedIn search

Recruiters searching LinkedIn Recruiter filter by keywords. Include the specific job titles, skills, and tools you want to be found for — naturally, as part of real sentences, not as a keyword dump at the bottom. "I specialize in Salesforce CRM implementation" works. "#Salesforce #CRM #implementation #B2B" at the end of your About section looks like spam.
