How to Write a Product Description That Sells

By the Ignitool team July 16, 2026 8 min read

Most product descriptions do one thing: describe the product. The best product descriptions do something harder — they make the reader feel what it would be like to own it. That shift in approach is the difference between a listing that converts and one that gets scrolled past.

Why most product descriptions fail to convert

The most common product description mistake is leading with features instead of benefits. Features describe what a product is. Benefits describe what the product does for the buyer — how it makes their life better, easier, more enjoyable, or more impressive.

❌ Feature-focused — weak conversion

Handmade soy wax candle. 8oz glass jar. Lavender and eucalyptus scent. 60-hour burn time. Lead-free cotton wick. Made in the USA.

✅ Benefit-focused — stronger conversion

Fill any room with the calming scent of lavender and eucalyptus — the combination aromatherapists recommend for winding down after a long day. Hand-poured in small batches from 100% natural soy wax, this candle burns cleanly for up to 60 hours without the black soot that paraffin candles leave behind. A genuinely better candle for people who care what they burn.

Both descriptions are about the same candle. The second one sells it because it answers the buyer's unspoken question: what does this do for me?

The product description formula that works

High-converting product descriptions follow a consistent structure regardless of the product category or platform:

The 5-part product description structure
1
The hook — Open with the primary benefit or the emotional outcome the buyer wants. Not what the product is — what it does.
2
The problem it solves — Briefly name the pain point or frustration your product eliminates. Buyers who recognize their problem immediately become more engaged.
3
Key features as benefits — List 3-5 features, but frame each one as a benefit. "60-hour burn time" becomes "burns for 60 hours — one candle lasts through an entire month of evening routines."
4
Credibility or social proof — A material detail, a certification, a production method, or a customer result that makes the claim believable.
5
The close — A subtle call to action or a final image that helps the buyer visualize ownership.

How to write for different platforms

Shopify and WooCommerce

Product pages on your own store give you the most space and control. Use a medium to long description (150-300 words) that covers the full formula above. Break up text with short paragraphs and bullet points for scannability. Your SEO depends on unique, keyword-rich descriptions — never copy manufacturer descriptions.

Etsy

Etsy buyers are emotionally driven — they want to know the story behind what they're buying. Lead with the human element: who made it, how it was made, and why it matters. Etsy descriptions should feel personal and warm, not like a retail catalog. Include all relevant details buyers need to make a decision: dimensions, materials, care instructions, and shipping time.

Amazon

Amazon descriptions need to work hard for SEO. Include your primary keyword in the first sentence. Use bullet points for the five key features — Amazon shoppers scan, they don't read. Keep bullet points benefit-focused and under 200 characters each. The A+ content section (available to brand-registered sellers) allows longer, richer descriptions that significantly improve conversion rates.

The words that sell and the words that don't

Certain words and phrases appear in high-converting product descriptions across categories. Others are so overused they've become invisible — or worse, they actively reduce trust.

Words that convert

  • Sensory words — smooth, velvety, crisp, warm, lightweight, sturdy. Help buyers feel the product through the screen.
  • Specific numbers — "lasts 60 hours" beats "long-lasting." "fits sizes 6-14" beats "available in multiple sizes."
  • You and your — write directly to the reader. "You'll love how..." converts better than "customers love how..."
  • Because — explaining why something matters increases persuasion. "Lead-free wick, because what burns in your home matters."

Words that undermine trust

  • Best, greatest, most amazing — superlatives without evidence are dismissed instantly by experienced online shoppers.
  • High quality — this phrase has been so overused it communicates nothing. Show quality through specific details instead.
  • Perfect for — weak opener. Lead with the benefit, not a generic use case.
  • Various colors available — list the actual colors. Vagueness creates friction.

How long should a product description be?

Length depends entirely on the product and platform. A $9 impulse purchase needs less persuasion than a $300 considered purchase. As a general guide:

  • Short (50-75 words) — impulse buys, low-price items, listing pages where many products appear together
  • Medium (100-150 words) — most standard ecommerce products, Etsy listings, mid-price items
  • Long (200-300 words) — high-ticket items, complex products, Amazon main listings, products that need education to convert

The rule: use as many words as the buyer needs to feel confident purchasing — no more, no fewer.

Product descriptions and SEO

Every product description on your site is an SEO asset. Unique descriptions help your products rank in Google Shopping and organic search. Duplicate descriptions — copied from manufacturers or used across multiple products — are actively penalized by Google and reduce your store's overall search visibility.

Include your primary keyword naturally in the first 50 words, use related terms throughout, and write descriptions long enough for Google to understand what the product is and who it's for. A well-written 150-word description outperforms a keyword-stuffed 50-word description every time.
